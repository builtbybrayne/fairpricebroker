# Recruiter demo — build spec and outreach

## Format

An interactive guided demo that steps through the full two-person workflow. The recruiter plays both sides — first as the recruiter, then as the candidate. Directed questions and comment boxes are embedded at each stage. Form submissions happen progressively so abandoned flows still yield data. Hosting on Netlify.

## Flow and framing copy

### Stage 1 — Recruiter enters employer budget

You're about to play both sides of the workflow — first as the recruiter, then as the candidate. This is a demo, so use any numbers you like.

In real use, this is where you enter the salary budget your client has given you for the role. This stays private — the candidate never sees it.

**Directed questions:**
- Do you typically know the employer's full salary budget at this stage of the process?
- Would you feel comfortable entering a client's budget into a third-party tool?

### Stage 2 — Candidate link sent

In real use, you'd send the candidate a private link at this point — before or instead of the salary conversation on your first call. They complete their side independently.

Now switch hats. You're the candidate. A recruiter has sent you this link and asked you to answer a few quick questions about your salary expectations.

**Directed questions:**
- Would you send this before or after a first call with the candidate?
- How likely do you think a candidate would be to complete this?
- What might make a candidate suspicious of or reluctant to use it?

### Stage 3 — Candidate answers the questions

The questions are framed so that answering honestly is in your interest as a candidate. Naming a lower floor doesn't hurt you — it signals that you're open to the right opportunity and increases your chances of a match.

Answer as a candidate would, based on whatever role you're imagining.

**Directed questions:**
- Does the argument that naming a lower salary earns more matches feel convincing to you?
- Do you think candidates would answer honestly?

### Stage 4 — Overlap output

This is what the recruiter sees. Two things: how aligned the salary overlap is, and whether non-remuneration factors — flexibility, equity, culture, purpose — need to be meaningfully in play to make this work.

This is the signal that replaces the standoff.

**Directed questions:**
- Is this output clear enough to act on?
- Does knowing whether non-salary factors need to be in play change how you'd approach this placement?
- Does this give you something you couldn't get from a phone call?

### Stage 5 — End of flow

That's the full workflow. Before you go, we have a few quick questions about what you just experienced.

**Directed questions:**
- Would you use this in your current workflow?
- What's your biggest hesitation?
- Would you pay for this — and if so, what model makes sense: per placement, monthly subscription, or credits?

## Outreach messages

### Direct LinkedIn message (semi-warm contacts)

> Hey [name], hope things are good. I'm building something aimed at the salary mismatch problem — the standoff where neither side wants to name a number first. I'd love to know if it resonates with how you work. It's a 3-minute interactive demo, no forms to fill in upfront. Would you be up for taking a look?
