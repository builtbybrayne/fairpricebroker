# Competitive landscape for fairprice.broker: clear white space, but the moat is thin

**No existing product does what fairprice.broker proposes — a lightweight, two-sided tool where both recruiter and candidate privately submit salary numbers and each sees only a mutual overlap signal.** Across direct competitors, benchmarking data providers, compensation-management platforms, ATS/workflow tools, and niche startups, the research found nothing that computes and returns a shared "in range / stretch / no overlap" signal from numbers submitted by both sides. The market is dense with *employer-side* comp infrastructure and a handful of tools that capture candidate expectations, but the specific privacy-preserving, symmetric overlap output is genuinely unoccupied. The catch: the concept's closest analogs are features bolted onto much larger platforms (Greenhouse, Levels.fyi, Barley, Deel), and the gap they leave is a feature gap, not a category gap — meaning the white space is real but not defensible on the idea alone. No source found any application of Van Westendorp / price-sensitivity methodology to salary matching, in either commercial products or academic literature [sawtoothsoftware](https://sawtoothsoftware.com/resources/blog/posts/van-westendorp-pricing-sensitivity-meter) [researchgate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/304658564_The_Van_Westendorp_Price-Sensitivity_Meter_As_A_Direct_Measure_Of_Willingness-To-Pay), which is either a true first-mover opportunity or a signal the method doesn't transfer to adversarial two-sided bargaining.

## Category 1 — Direct competitors: the space is empty, but three products sit adjacent

No direct competitor exists. The closest analogs each capture *one* side's number or disclose salary, but none runs a two-sided private overlap computation.

- **Greenhouse "Compensation mismatch early warning"** is the closest comparison-based feature found: it flags candidates whose stated desired salary exceeds the **midpoint of the posted salary band** [greenhouse](https://support.greenhouse.io/hc/en-us/articles/52193605120155-Greenhouse-MCP-use-cases-by-role). But it is a **one-sided recruiter flag**, not a mutual signal shown to both parties, and it is described as an example workflow under **Greenhouse MCP, which is in open beta** — not a native, generally-available UI feature [greenhouse](https://support.greenhouse.io/hc/en-us/articles/52193605120155-Greenhouse-MCP-use-cases-by-role).
- **Dex (meetdex.ai)** is an AI recruiting marketplace marketing "salary shown upfront, always" and "full salary and package transparency before any conversation" [meetdex](https://meetdex.ai/get-dex). But it is salary *disclosure* plus AI matching, not a mutual numeric overlap. Its pricing is agency-shaped: a **20–30% first-year-salary success fee** [thenextweb](https://thenextweb.com/news/ai-recruiting-startup-dex-raises-5-3m-seed-to-match-ai-engineers-with-the-companies-that-need-them-most).
- Lightweight candidate-side calculators (PayPeek at **$10/mo**, HireFlow's free salary calculator, ATZ CRM's recruiter-side benchmark calculator) exist but are single-sided estimation tools, not overlap screens [paypeek](https://paypeek.ai/) [hireflow](https://hireflow.net/salary-calculator) [atzcrm](https://atzcrm.com/tools/salary-benchmark-calculator/).

**Gap fairprice.broker fills:** the symmetric, private, two-sided overlap output shown to *both* sides is unoccupied. The Van Westendorp framing has no precedent in this domain — genuine novelty, with the associated risk that novelty in an adversarial negotiation context may be unproven for a reason.

But it exposes the raw number to the recruiter and doesn't give the candidate a private "are we in range" signal — fairprice.broker's privacy-preserving symmetry (neither side sees the other's figure, only overlap) is the differentiator.

## Category 4 — ATS / workflow tools: budget guardrails and salary-expectation fields are common; the mutual overlap is not

Every major ATS can (a) capture a candidate's salary expectation as a structured field and (b) enforce employer-side budget/band guardrails at offer stage — but as **separate, one-sided mechanisms**, not a joint overlap signal.

- **Greenhouse:** offer exceptions requiring approval outside allowed salary ranges [greenhouse](https://support.greenhouse.io/hc/en-us/articles/204168849-Offer-exceptions-using-a-custom-job-field); captures current/desired salary as structured fields [greenhouse](https://support.greenhouse.io/hc/en-us/articles/360024688071-Link-custom-job-post-question-to-custom-field-on-candidate-profile); and the beta MCP "mismatch early warning" flag noted above. Pricing quote-based, est. **$8k–$80k/yr** by tier [herohunt](https://www.herohunt.ai/blog/greenhouse-recruiting-full-pricing-guide-2026).
- **Ashby:** natively adjusts candidate *leveling* by combining "compensation expectations and the seniority of the role" — a two-sided reconciliation, but for leveling, not a shared overlap output [ashbyhq](https://www.ashbyhq.com/blog/engineering/leveling-and-compensation). From **~$400/mo** [avahr](https://avahr.com/ashby-pricing/).
- **Lever:** conditional offer approvals when comp falls outside an approved band [lever](https://www.lever.co/blog/job-offer-approval-process/); captures salary expectations via Screened integration [lever](https://help.lever.co/hc/en-us/articles/21614057853341-Enabling-and-using-the-Screened-integration). Est. **$6–8/employee/mo** [dover](https://www.dover.com/blog/lever-reviews-pricing-alternatives).
- **Workable:** "Expected Salary" custom field + filtering to disqualify candidates whose expectations exceed budget [workable](https://help.workable.com/hc/en-us/articles/23365968258711-Best-practices-Candidate-Screening). From **$299/mo** [selectsoftwarereviews](https://www.selectsoftwarereviews.com/buyer-guide/applicant-tracking-systems).
- **Rippling:** auto-populates requisition salary ranges and blocks over-budget reqs — employer-side only, no candidate expectation field found [rippling](https://www.rippling.com/blog/rippling-recruiting-review).

**Implication:** the raw ingredients of fairprice.broker (a candidate expectation field + an employer band + a comparison) already exist inside ATS platforms recruiters use daily. Greenhouse's beta MCP flag shows an incumbent is already experimenting with exactly the comparison logic. This is the **strongest structural threat**: the feature is a plausible near-term addition for any ATS, and agency recruiters — the target buyer — often work in Bullhorn, where no native comp-alignment feature was found [bullhorn](https://kb.bullhorn.com/bh4sf/Content/BH4SF/Topics/bh4sfGlossary.htm), leaving a real gap in the *agency* segment specifically.

## Category 5 — Lightweight/standalone tools: knockout filters, not overlap tools

The lightweight end is occupied by one-sided mechanisms: ATS "knockout questions" on salary [neuracv](https://neuracv.com/resources/blog/ats-assessment-test-2026), Jarvi's AI notetaker extracting salary expectations from screening calls as recruiter-side knockout criteria [jarvi](https://www.jarvi.tech/en/ats/automated-candidate-screening/), and automated pre-filters routing candidates whose expectation exceeds ~130% of the range [ustechautomations](https://ustechautomations.com/resources/blog/recruiting-candidate-screening-how-to-2026). All are employer-side gating. No lightweight *two-sided* form or calculator was found.

## The white space and the threat, side by side

**The clearest white space** is a narrow but real positioning: a *privacy-preserving, symmetric, two-sided* overlap gate where neither party sees the other's number — only overlap level plus a non-pay-factor trigger. Every incumbent either (a) keeps salary numbers employer-side, or (b) discloses the candidate's raw number to the recruiter (Levels.fyi Talent Pool, Barley). None gives the *candidate* a private "are we in range" signal, which is the piece that could make honest candidate input rational rather than adversarial. The Van Westendorp framing is unprecedented in this domain — a defensible novelty hook for marketing, though unproven in practice.

**The strongest incumbent threats**, ranked:
1. **ATS platforms adding the comparison natively** — Greenhouse already has the candidate field, the employer band, offer guardrails, and a beta mismatch flag [greenhouse](https://support.greenhouse.io/hc/en-us/articles/52193605120155-Greenhouse-MCP-use-cases-by-role). The overlap signal is a small increment away, and distribution is already in place.
2. **Levels.fyi Talent Pool** — closest on two-sided *data*, already exposes candidate expectations to recruiters [levels](https://www.levels.fyi/offerings/talent), though it lacks the privacy-preserving symmetry.
3. **Barley Talent Pulse / Deel** — capturing or calibrating candidate expectations, one step from an overlap feature [chromewebstore](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/barley-talent-pulse/mengckbicigchenmmcmdjfmkmahpggpk) [deel](https://www.deel.com/solutions/compensation/).

The decision this points to: fairprice.broker occupies real white space, but the moat is the *specific two-sided private framing and the agency-recruiter segment* — not the underlying comparison, which is trivially replicable and already appearing in incumbents. The wedge is strongest where incumbents are weakest: **standalone, agency-recruiter-facing, and privacy-symmetric**, since agency ATS platforms (Bullhorn) lack native comp alignment and the big comp platforms are built for in-house employer comp teams, not third-party recruiters.

## Where further research would most change the conclusion

Two gaps remain. First, **Greenhouse MCP's trajectory** — whether the "compensation mismatch early warning" graduates from open beta to a native, default feature would materially raise the incumbent threat, since it is the single closest comparison-logic product and rests on one support-doc source. Second, **the agency-recruiter tooling stack specifically** (Bullhorn, JobAdder, iCIMS and their app marketplaces): confirming that none offers a native two-sided comp gate would firm up the claim that the agency segment is the cleanest white space, since this report's ATS coverage skewed toward in-house-oriented platforms (Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby).
